# Writing standards

Use the mode that matches the requested output. These are quality checks, not a test for human origin. A word or pattern is acceptable when it is precise, intentional, and suited to the voice.

## Shared checks

- Start with the useful information. Remove greetings, meta-introductions, praise, and wrap-up language when they add no value.
- Prefer concrete verbs, exact nouns, and specific relationships. Review repeated or inflated wording such as `delve`, `leverage`, `utilize`, `seamless`, `robust`, `multifaceted`, `pivotal`, `foster`, `underscore`, `tapestry`, `landscape`, and `in today's fast-paced world`. Replace a term only when the replacement keeps the meaning.
- Replace vague claims and prestige labels such as `multifaceted`, `crucial`, `compelling`, `significant`, `innovative`, and `transformative` with evidence, examples, limits, dates, or named sources. Keep the label only when the text defines and supports it.
- Replace indirect forms such as `serves as` or `stands as` with `is` when that is the intended meaning. Keep hedging when the evidence is uncertain. Remove stacked hedges.
- Replace contrast formulas such as `not X, but Y` with a direct statement when the contrast adds no real reasoning. Avoid repeated triplets and uniform bold-label lists.
- Match rhythm to the work. Vary sentence length and paragraph shape without imposing a quota. Preserve fragments, repetition, dialect, and other deliberate choices when they carry voice.
- Keep headings, bullets, quotes, code, citations, and links in the format the audience needs. Remove accidental assistant or platform metadata.

## Academic writing

- Separate observation, interpretation, and speculation. Preserve the strength of each claim.
- Use only citations supplied by the user or verified from the available sources. Never invent authors, papers, DOIs, page numbers, or findings. Mark missing support as `[citation needed: topic]`.
- Keep methods and terms exact. Prefer active voice when it names the actor clearly; use passive voice when the action matters more than the actor. If a label such as `multifaceted` remains, name its dimensions.
- Remove paper-tour introductions and unsupported significance. Use sentence-case headings and restrained lists.

## Creative writing

- Preserve genre, point of view, tense, voice, pacing, and stated story facts. Do not add plot events to make prose seem less formulaic.
- Favor concrete sensory detail, character choice, subtext, and consequence over abstract explanation. Review lip-biting, sighing, hair-touching, eye-locking, shrugging, and similar stock gestures. Keep one only when it is character-specific and earns its place.
- Trust the reader. When a passage ends with a theme, moral, or emotional explanation, delete or recast that sentence rather than paraphrasing it. End on a concrete action, image, or unanswered detail when the story earns it.
- Use time shifts, rough grammar, fragments, and uneven pacing only when they serve the story.

## Technical writing

- Apply ASD-STE100 when the user requests Simplified Technical English. Use one action or idea per sentence or step, active voice, imperative steps, complete words, and condition-before-action order.
- Avoid noun stacks and vague action verbs. Replace `synchronize` with `copy`, `match`, or another exact action when the direction is known. State exact prerequisites, limits, units, causes, errors, warnings, recovery steps, and expected results.
- Keep commands, identifiers, code, and product terms exact. Do not replace a technical term with a plainer word when precision would suffer.

## Email, chat, support, and outreach

- Put the reason, answer, or ask in the first useful line. Match the relationship and channel.
- Keep chat brief and focused on one idea. Use a clear action or decision.
- For support and incidents, state time, impact, cause if known, action taken, and the next customer action. Own the problem without empty apology.
- For outreach, use only verified signals and a specific offer. Do not invent personalization or ask for a meeting without a clear reason.

## Social and B2B copy

- Open with a concrete result, problem, observation, or verified data point. State one clear point of view.
- Replace hype with proof, limits, trade-offs, and useful detail. Do not invent metrics, customer stories, or urgency.
- Keep formatting light. Use readable paragraphs or irregular lists instead of repeated bold-label bullets and decorative emoji. Make the call to action specific and low effort.

## Neutral reference writing

- Use a neutral point of view. State facts directly and attribute opinions to named sources.
- Remove praise, promotional wording, legacy claims, vague reception claims, and broad significance unless specific sources support them.
- Keep terms consistent. Do not rotate synonyms for style. End with the final relevant fact instead of an upbeat summary.

## Final pass

Read the result aloud once. Check that each sentence earns its place, each claim has the right certainty, and each instruction or request is clear. If the source lacks a fact needed for a safe or accurate rewrite, stop and flag the gap.
